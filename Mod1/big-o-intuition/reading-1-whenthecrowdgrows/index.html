When the Crowd Grows

Imagine you're checking wristbands at a festival. With 8 guests, almost any method feels fast. With 80,000 guests, the way you find one person suddenly matters. Big-O is language for describing how the work grows.

10. Required bridge to lecture/lab

You just built the intuition that O(1) means cost stays roughly flat as data grows, while O(n) means cost grows with the number of items. You also practiced the most important misconception check: Big-O describes growth, not a stopwatch verdict.

In lecture, you'll put formal names onto these patterns, reason about larger scales, and practice defending why a lookup choice matters.
